## The short answer

Would a rebuild differ? Yes, and the biggest changes are decisions about evidence and scope, not about the stack or the code shapes. What was built mostly held: Avalonia plus SkiaSharp gave a Windows-only show tool a headless whole-desk suite on Linux; `BarsPattern.cs` is byte-identical apart from the round-59 namespace move and the 13 day-1 pixel asserts still pass through the real engine (three of the seven day-1 renderer files did grow substantially); the show file lost no operator's show through ten schema bumps; and the one action layer is what let the first real fault from a show be pinned to the second in the journal and fixed with a regression test in one round.

Five things. First, the show laptop's files (Super Check, `patterns.log`, `patterns.showlog.jsonl`, the recovery record) reached the repository together at round 71 of 78 and as a full set at rounds 77 and 78; each read found a class of fault the 2,429 headless tests structurally could not see, including a per-second COM enumeration on the UI thread present since round 9, and eleven TAKE presses journaled Done with the outputs off. Nineteen rig checklists and 21 qualification matrices were written; none has a filled row. Second, about 13,400 source lines of twin failover, nodes, arcade and audience phones (by the distributed lens's classification) were built between 09-12 and 09-14, before one machine had a qualified row; none of it has run on two real machines, and the project wrote its own gate for that work at round 75, after building it. Third, the edited show and the on-air programme are the same class, told apart by one null-coalescing property; readers of the wrong copy recurred from 31 August to round 72, and the round-78 field fault sat at the edge of the same class, a rule that read the edited state's own flag where the programme's was meant but that needed both states and the editing target to be right. Fourth, `ActionResult` is a status plus words, so the record could not contradict an attempt; the doctrine "attempts are not facts" had to be re-applied area by area from round 43 to 78. Fifth, the desk's own cost was governed before it was measured: about 40 budget, ladder, fence and governor classes and their reading surfaces against two allocation tests, while the field's render clock ran at 12 to 16 Hz under a clip-plus-web show whose CPU load was the web chain and decode, with every desk miniature a full engine pass on the same compositor thread and the ladder governing the wrong variable.

The single largest change is about evidence and scope, not code, and it has four parts: the desk's own files with every report from the first laptop build (the maintainer had the laptop from 08-30, as `0d9ccfb`'s field-feedback round shows, the day the switcher commits landed, so "a v1 qualified before the switcher" was never on offer); a short scripted walk on every calendar day a build is picked up; the standing brief's closing sentence naming the rig, rather than the wire and Companion, as what each step considers; and the twin and node asks answered "viable, not now" until one machine had a recorded run. Breadth capped at what the rig had proven is the intended effect, and entry 2 prices it in lines and hours rather than rounds: about one calendar day of 21. The maintainer's own round-56 brief said the scope half ("leave any work on the twin machine until later; we need to concentrate on getting a single machine rock solid", PLAN §74), and that round immediately found the show serialised three times per GO on the UI thread.
